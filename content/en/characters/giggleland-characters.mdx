## What the Wiki Entries Reveal — and What They Don't

A category page is an index, not a character bible. It tells you which names exist in the project's documentation; it does not tell you who those people are, when they appear, or what they want. Treating an index entry as a full profile is the single most common way fan write-ups end up wrong.

| Data point | Available on the category page? | What that means for you |
|------------|-------------------------------|--------------------------|
| Canonical spelling | Yes, for every listed entry | Safe to cite the name as written |
| Story role or personality | No | Do not assign traits without a source |
| First appearance | No | Check the individual character page |
| Voice cast or credits | No | Verify through official channels |
| Character art | Partial — two of nine entries | Absence of art is not absence of a character |
| Relationships to other characters | No | Anything you read elsewhere is unverified |
| Aliases | Only where shown, e.g. Mr. T | Search both names to avoid dead ends |

The practical takeaway: use the category page to confirm spelling and existence, then move to the individual page for substance. If the individual page is a stub, say so in your own writing rather than filling the gap with guesswork. Readers forgive an honest "details are still emerging" far more readily than they forgive a confident invention.

One more caution applies specifically to the U entry. "User-jgrherah" follows a username convention, not a character convention, which means the category has picked up at least one page that does not belong to the cast at all. ## How to Verify Giggleland Characters Details Yourself

Verification does not require special access. It requires a fixed order of operations, so you do not accidentally treat a fan summary as a primary source.

1. **Start with the category index.** Confirm the name exists and note the exact spelling, including any parenthetical alias.
2. **Open the individual character page.** Read the page itself rather than the category blurb; that is where any role, appearance, or relationship detail would live.
3. **Check the page history and last-edited date.** A page edited yesterday is more likely to reflect current information than one untouched for a year.
4. **Cross-reference official channels.** Store pages, developer posts, and trailers outrank fan summaries whenever the two disagree.
5. **Record your access date.** Wiki content changes; a date stamp lets readers judge how fresh your information is.

| Source type | Relative reliability | Best use |
|-------------|---------------------|----------|
| Official store page or developer post | Highest | Confirming names, release info, official art |
| Community wiki character pages | Good, with caveats | Roster, spelling, compiled details |
| Community wiki category index | Directional only | Confirming a name exists |
| Video comments and forum threads | Low | Leads to investigate, not citations |
| AI-generated summaries and aggregators | Lowest | Never a sole source |

For step four, the quickest official check is to [search Steam for Giggleland](https://store.steampowered.com/search/?term=Giggleland) and compare any listed cast or store description against what the wiki claims. If the store page is silent on a name, that silence is itself information — it means the name is community-documented rather than officially announced.

## Where Community Reports Fit In

Player experience fills gaps that indexes cannot. Community reports are often the first place a new name surfaces, and they are frequently correct — but they arrive without the structure that makes a fact checkable.

- **Treat early name sightings as leads.** A name posted in a discussion thread is a prompt to go look, not a conclusion.
- **Separate observation from interpretation.** "This character appears in this scene" is an observation. "This character is the villain" is an interpretation, and interpretations spread faster than evidence.
- **Watch for name drift.** Fan shorthand often shortens or respells a name, and the shorthand can outrun the official spelling in search results.
- **Ask for the source behind the claim.** A screenshot, a timestamp, or a link is verifiable. A confident tone is not.
- **Expect churn.** Rosters on young projects change. Anything you publish about the Giggleland characters should be easy to update later.

A useful habit is keeping a simple personal log: name, where you saw it, the date, and your confidence level. Three or four entries into that log, patterns appear — you will notice which names keep resurfacing and which ones appeared once and vanished. That log becomes the backbone of any article, video, or wiki contribution you make.
