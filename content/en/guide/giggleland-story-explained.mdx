## How to Follow the Giggleland Story Explained Without Falling for Fake Leaks

The single biggest mistake new fans make is treating a well-edited video as a source. It is not. A source is a developer post, an official trailer, a store page, or a verified studio account.

| Evidence tier | Example | How to treat it |
|---|---|---|
| Tier 1 | Official trailer, store page, developer statement | Fact — build your timeline here |
| Tier 2 | Verified studio social posts, official ARG accounts | Strong signal — still incomplete |
| Tier 3 | Frame-by-frame community analysis | Useful interpretation, not fact |
| Tier 4 | Anonymous "leaks," Discord screenshots | Entertainment only |

Three practical rules that save a lot of heartache:

- **Screenshot everything.** Teasers get deleted, and a theory without a source image is unverifiable within a week.
- **Date your evidence.** A clue from eight months ago may already be contradicted by a newer trailer.
- **Check the account.** Fan-made ARG accounts mimic official ones convincingly. Look for verification history, not just a matching logo.

Community reports also flag a recurring problem: theories get repeated so often that they start to feel official. Repetition is not confirmation. When a claim circulates without a Tier 1 or Tier 2 source, file it as folklore.
